A clear promise, a real product shown in the hero, and strong proof. The page now speaks to two audiences at once, health systems and every business, and the two calls to action compete with each other.

First screen on a 1440 px laptop, 15 Nov 2026

What's working

  • The headline promise is concrete: Get the margin you negotiated.
  • The hero shows the actual product, an invoice flagged against its contract, instead of an abstract illustration.
  • Credible proof: health-system logos, a quote from OSF HealthCare's Chief Supply Chain Officer, SOC 2 and HIPAA. And it loads fast (main content in about 1.6 s on a phone).

Three fixes,
in priority order

1Pick one main path for the first two screens

What I saw
The hero asks health systems to Book a demo. The very next section says Now every business can use it and asks for a waitlist sign-up (Reserve your spot).
Why it matters
A hospital CFO and a general business owner need different next steps. Two primary actions back to back split attention, and the waitlist can make the product feel unfinished to the health-system buyer you already serve.
Suggested fix
Keep the first screens for the buyer you sell to today. Move the waitlist to its own page, or add a simple choice (Health system / Other business) that sends each visitor to the right action.

2Put your number in the hero

What I saw
Prevents 3–8% in overpayments is the most persuasive line on the page, but it sits about ten screens down. The page is about 12,800 px tall on a laptop and 10,800 px on a phone.
Why it matters
Finance buyers decide on numbers, and most visitors never scroll that far.
Suggested fix
Add the 3–8% figure to the subline or a proof strip under the hero, and trim the page so the case is made in fewer screens.

3Shorten the headline

What I saw
The H1 is 17 words across two sentences, with the second sentence greyed out.
Why it matters
The first sentence is the hook. The second reads like a subline but carries headline weight, so the eye has to work harder.
Suggested fix
Keep Get the margin you negotiated. as the headline and move Turn complex contracts into enforceable controls before payment into the paragraph below.

Also: At laptop width the dark SpendMail section is mostly empty space around a short paragraph. A product visual there would carry the idea faster.
