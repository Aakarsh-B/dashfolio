Homepage audit · fluxcap.com · 15 Nov 2026

FluxCap

A distinctive brand that already stands apart from typical legal tech. The gaps are about saying what FluxCap is, and who it's for, fast enough for a busy litigator, and putting your strongest proof where it's seen.

Prepared by Aakarsh B, brand and web designer for SaaS teams

First screen on a 1440 px laptop, 15 Nov 2026

What's working

  • The legal-pad yellow and editorial type feel like the world of litigation, not generic AI software. It's memorable.
  • It's fast: on a phone the main content shows in about 1.7 s, with roughly 300 KB downloaded.
  • One clear action. Request a demo is the only call to action, so nobody wonders what to do next.

Three fixes,
in priority order

1Say "antitrust" in the first screen

What I saw
The headline is Built to Play Offense, and the paragraph talks about "consequential cases" and "enforcers". The words antitrust and litigation don't appear above the fold. Antitrust first shows up further down, in the FluxAlmanac card (Sherman Act section 2).
Why it matters
Visitors arriving from your launch coverage, a referral or search should know within a few seconds that this is built for antitrust and complex litigation teams. A clever headline works best with a plain line next to it.
Suggested fix
Keep the headline and add a short eyebrow or subline, for example: AI built for antitrust and complex litigation teams.

2Move your strongest proof up

What I saw
Currently being used in active matters with damages in excess of $1 billion and 140+ years combined legal experience sit near the bottom of the page. Built by the lawyers who helped shape the law doesn't name anyone.
Why it matters
For this buyer, who built FluxCap is the most persuasive thing on the page. Your founders' Justice Department antitrust leadership was widely covered at launch, but the homepage never says it.
Suggested fix
Add a slim proof strip right under the hero: the $1B+ matters line, the founders' former roles, and your backers (Y Combinator, Burford Capital, Bloomberg Beta).

3Show the product near the top

What I saw
The first screen is a staged photo. The three engines (FluxAlmanac, FluxFacts, Vision Manager) appear only after scrolling, and they animate in as you scroll: in a full-page capture without scrolling, those sections are blank.
Why it matters
Lawyers evaluating a tool want to see the workspace. Content that only appears on scroll is also missed by anything that captures the page in one go, such as screenshot tools and some link previewers.
Suggested fix
Put a cropped shot of the FluxAlmanac view in or just under the hero, and let sections start visible and animate from that visible state rather than from hidden.

Also: The page title is just FluxCap. Something like FluxCap | AI for antitrust litigation helps search results and browser tabs.
